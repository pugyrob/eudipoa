**Before you can automate "may this person do this thing for that company", you need the authority itself as data. Not a PDF, not a boolean on a user record: a mandate with a grantor, a grantee, a scope, a type, a validity window and a status.** This page describes the shape we use in practice. Where the law fixes a field, we cite it. Everywhere else this is experience, not obligation.

## Why a document is not enough

A signed power of attorney answers one question: was this granted properly? Operations ask a different question, repeatedly: is this action, by this person, for this company, allowed **now**? To answer that at machine speed the document's content has to become structure. The law already commits to a minimum structure: the template for the digital EU power of attorney must contain "at least the data fields relating to the scope of representation, the person authorised to represent the company and the type of representation" <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(4)" />. Those three fields are the start of the mandate object.

## The fields, and where they come from

| Field | Why it exists | Legal anchor, where one exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grantor | The person whose identity, capacity and authority to represent were verified at granting | Verification floor in <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(1), second subpara." /> |
| Principal (the company) | The organisation being bound; carries register identity (EUID) | Company identification in the EU Company Certificate <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(2)(c)–(d)" /> |
| Grantee (the authorised person) | Who may act | Template field: "the person authorised to represent the company" <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(4)" /> |
| Scope of representation | Which procedures/actions are covered — the acceptance rule stops at "as specified in the document" | <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(2) and 16c(4)" /> |
| Type of representation | Sole, joint, or otherwise qualified | Template field <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(4)" />; alone-or-jointly is register-grade data for original representatives <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(2)(j)" /> |
| Status + lifecycle events | Granted, amended, revoked — each a verified national-law act | <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(1) and (3)" /> |
| Evidence trail | Which authority verified, when; where filed, if filed | Filing option in <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16c(3)" /> |

Two additions the law does not require but practice does: a **validity window** (open-ended mandates accumulate risk; short-lived, renewable ones bound it) and a **constraint set** on the scope (value limits, counterparty limits, procedure limits). Scope written as prose gets read once by a human. Scope written as structure gets checked by a system every time.

## Lifecycle, not lookup

The expensive modelling mistake is treating authority as a lookup: `is_authorised: true`. Authority is a lifecycle. It is granted by someone whose own authority was checked, possibly amended, possibly revoked, and where the qualified-attestation rule applies the revocation is valid "from the moment of its revocation" and irreversible <Cite id="reg-2024-1183" pin="Art. 45d(4) of Reg. 910/2014 as inserted" />. A mandate object needs its history attached, because you will be asked not only "may they act?" but "were they allowed to act on 14 March?". Only the event log answers that.

## What this buys you when the law arrives

When the Commission's template is published (it was due by 31 July 2026 <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 24(2)(e)" />) and wallets start carrying attestations of "powers and mandates to represent natural or legal persons" <Cite id="reg-2024-1183" pin="Annex VI, point 9" />, systems that already hold mandates as structured records can take the attestation as evidence into an existing model. Systems that hold PDFs will be parsing prose against a deadline.
