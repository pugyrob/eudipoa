**The EU Company Certificate is a harmonised, register-issued certificate — electronic or paper — that every Member State must accept as sufficient evidence, at the time of issuance, of a company's incorporation and of the register information it lists. Companies can get it free of charge at least once a year.** It is created by a new Article 16b of Directive (EU) 2017/1132, inserted by Directive (EU) 2025/25.

## Who this page is for

Companies that need to prove what they are in another Member State without an apostille and a certified translation; authorities and relying parties deciding what to accept; register operators implementing issuance.

## What the law says

**Issued by registers, accepted everywhere.** Member States must ensure their registers issue EU Company Certificates for the companies listed in Annexes II and IIB of Directive (EU) 2017/1132. The certificate "shall be accepted in all Member States as sufficient evidence, at the time of its issuance, of the incorporation of the company" and of the listed register information <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(1)" />.

**What it contains.** For limited liability companies, Article 16b(2) lists the contents: name(s); legal form; registration number and Member State; EUID; registered office; correspondence address; date of registration; subscribed capital (if applicable); status (closed, struck off, wound up, dissolved, in insolvency, active or inactive, where recorded); the persons authorised to represent the company in dealings with third parties and in legal proceedings — with whether they act alone or jointly — including, where the representative is a legal person, its identifying details; the object of the company (where recorded); its duration (where limited); website (where recorded); and the certificate's date of issue <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(2)(a)–(o)" />. For partnerships listed in Annex IIB, the same applies minus registered office, capital and the representative-person points <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(3)" />.

The representation field is the important one: the certificate is a register-issued, cross-border-accepted statement of **who may bind the company**. That is why this handbook treats it as one half of the organisational-mandate story, alongside the [digital EU power of attorney](/poa). See [Who may act for a company](/mandates/who-may-act).

**How you get it.** From the register, electronically or on paper; the electronic version must also be obtainable through the system of interconnection of registers (BRIS) <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(4)" />.

**What it costs.** Each company can obtain its own certificate in electronic form free of charge, unless that causes serious prejudice to the financing of national registers — and in any case free at least once per calendar year. Any price charged, electronic or paper, is capped at administrative costs <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(5)" />. Third parties, including authorities, can also apply for a company's certificate <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="recital 24" />.

**How it is authenticated.** The electronic certificate is authenticated by trust services under Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 <Cite id="reg-910-2014" /> and "shall also be compatible with the European Digital Identity Wallet" <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(6)" /> <Cite id="reg-2024-1183" />. The paper version carries the date of issuance, the register's seal or stamp or equivalent, and a unique protocol or identification number allowing electronic verification of its origin and authenticity <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(7)" />.

**No apostille, limited translation demands.** The certificate is exempt from all forms of legalisation and any similar formality <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16d(2)" />, and Member States must endeavour not to demand translations where the needed information is in the certificate <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16g(1)(a)" />.

**When an authority doubts it.** The Article 16e safeguard applies: an authority with reasonable doubt about origin or authenticity queries a contact point; the contact point answers within 5 working days; refusal is allowed only if the issuing register does not confirm, with notification to the presenter within 10 working days <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16e(1)–(4)" />.

**The template.** A multilingual template, published by the Commission on the e-Justice Portal in all official EU languages; the implementing act — including wallet compatibility, taxonomy and template — is due by 31 July 2026 <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 16b(8) and Art. 24(2)(d)" />.

## What it does not do

- It proves the state of the register **at the time of issuance**; Article 16b(1) says exactly that. It is not a live feed. A certificate from March says nothing about a director change in April.
- It does not replace national extracts and certificates; the recitals keep those intact: "The EU Company Certificate should be without prejudice to national extracts and certificates" <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="recital 24" />.
- It does not itself authorise anyone to do anything. It states who the company's representatives are; conferring authority on someone else is the [power of attorney's](/poa) job.

## Dates

Same clock as the rest of the Directive: implementing acts by 31 July 2026; transposition by 31 July 2027; application from 31 July 2028 <Cite id="dir-2025-25" pin="Art. 24(2) insertion and Art. 4" />.
